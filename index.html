New here? Welcome to the clinic 🐰

A quick, no-pressure explainer if you're just starting to track your period.

What's actually happening?

A period is your body shedding the uterus lining once a month if pregnancy hasn't happened — it's a completely normal, healthy part of the reproductive cycle, not something to be embarrassed about.

The general numbers

  • First periods usually show up somewhere between ages 10–15, but everybody's timeline is different.
  • A "cycle" is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next — commonly around 21–35 days, with 28 being the textbook average.
  • The bleeding itself usually lasts about 3–7 days.
  • Irregular cycles are very common in the first couple years — it usually settles down over time.

What can help day-to-day

  • Pads, tampons, or menstrual cups are all normal options — it's personal preference, try what feels comfortable.
  • A heating pad or hot water bottle on the lower belly can ease cramps.
  • Staying hydrated and gentle movement (even just walking) can help with bloating and mood.
  • Keeping a few supplies in your bag removes a lot of the stress around surprise starts.

When to actually check in with a doctor

  • No period at all by around age 15–16.
  • Bleeding so heavy you're soaking through a pad/tampon every hour.
  • Pain that stops you from going about your day, even with rest or a heating pad.
  • Periods that suddenly become very irregular after being regular.
This is general info to help you get oriented, not medical advice — a doctor or gynecologist is always the right call for anything that worries you.
